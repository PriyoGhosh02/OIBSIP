## 🎯 1. Objective

The primary objective of this project is to craft a visually captivating, clean, and educational **Tribute Page** dedicated to the legendary theoretical physicist **Albert Einstein** as part of **Task 2 (Level 2)** of the **Oasis Infobyte (OIBSIP)** Web Development and Designing Internship.

Key goals include:
- Establishing a classic **editorial / museum publication aesthetic** emphasizing strong typography, graceful whitespace, and dignified color transitions.
- Structuring the content into semantic, accessible HTML5 sections (`<header>`, `<nav>`, `<main>`, `<section>`, `<article>`, `<aside>`, `<blockquote>`, `<footer>`).
- Presenting rigorously researched historical facts in **original paraphrased prose** without verbatim copying from external encyclopedias.
- Demonstrating multi-section background color transitions:
  - Hero Section: Warm Antique Off-White (`#F8F5EF`)
  - Biography Section: Crisp Editorial White (`#FFFFFF`)
  - Timeline Section: Soft Slate Blue-Gray (`#E8EEF5`)
  - Scientific Contributions: Crisp White (`#FFFFFF`)
  - Quote & Footer: Deep Rich Navy (`#172033` / `#0F1626`)
- Providing a smooth, distraction-free reading experience that adapts flawlessly from wide desktop displays down to compact mobile viewports.

---

## 💡 2. Motivation

Albert Einstein (1879–1955) transformed the paradigm of human physics. His formulation of Special and General Relativity unraveled centuries of Newtonian orthodoxy, demonstrating that time, space, and gravity are dynamically intertwined. His explanation of the photoelectric effect catalyzed the birth of quantum mechanics, while his lifelong advocacy for peace, civil liberties, and humanism cemented his status as a timeless cultural philosopher.

The motivation behind designing this digital tribute was to move away from cluttered, generic tribute pages and instead emulate the curated elegance of a **high-end literary magazine or digital museum exhibit**:
1. **Typographic Dignity**: Harmonious pairing of the distinguished serif typeface **Playfair Display** for high-impact headlines with the clean geometric sans-serif **Inter** for sustained readability.
2. **Visual Storytelling**: Integrating authentic, public-domain historical photography from Wikimedia Commons alongside clear architectural timeline milestones.
3. **Pure Craftsmanship**: 100% vanilla HTML5 and CSS3, achieving lightning-fast loading speeds, zero dependency overhead, and smooth anchor scrolling.

## 🏛️ 5. Section Breakdown & Content Guide

### 1. 🧭 Sticky Editorial Navigation Bar
- Monogram logo: Golden `AE` emblem accompanied by `Albert Einstein` in Playfair Display.
- Responsive anchor links: `Home`, `Biography`, `Timeline`, `Contributions`, `Quote`.
- Frosted glass effect (`backdrop-filter: blur(10px)`).

### 2. 🌟 Hero Section ("A Remarkable Scientist")
- Eyebrow badge: `A REMARKABLE SCIENTIST` in letter-spaced antique bronze.
- Headline: `Albert Einstein` with subtitle `A Mind That Changed Our Understanding of the Universe`.
- Credentials pill: `Physicist • Thinker • Nobel Prize Winner`.
- Historical tribute visual featuring chalkboard notes ($E = mc^2$) and call-to-action button `Explore His Story ↓`.

### 3. 📖 Biography Section ("About Albert Einstein")
Four original paragraphs covering:
1. **Early Curiosity**: Born March 14, 1879, in Ulm; early wonder sparked by a magnetic pocket compass.
2. **Swiss Education & Annus Mirabilis**: Studies in Zurich, the Swiss Patent Office, and the four landmark 1905 papers.
3. **General Theory of Relativity**: The 1915 breakthrough explaining gravity as spacetime curvature and the dramatic 1919 eclipse confirmation.
4. **Nobel Prize & Humanist Legacy**: 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics, immigration to Princeton in 1933, and worldwide humanitarian advocacy.
- Includes authentic historical photograph: *Albert Einstein at the blackboard in Pasadena (1931)* with Wikimedia Commons attribution.

### 4. ⏳ Timeline Section ("Key Moments in His Life")
Six major milestone cards arranged along a connected track:
- **1879 — Born in Ulm**: Birth in Ulm, German Empire.
- **1905 — Annus Mirabilis**: Publication of special relativity and photoelectric effect papers.
- **1915 — General Relativity**: Completion of the field equations of general relativity.
- **1921 — Nobel Prize**: Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for quantum explanation of light.
- **1933 — Moved to the U.S.**: Emigration to America and appointment at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study.
- **1955 — Passed Away**: Death in Princeton, New Jersey, leaving an indelible mark on science.

### 5. ⚛️ Scientific Contributions
Three focused editorial cards:
- **Special Relativity**: Uniform speed of light, unification of space and time, and mass-energy equivalence ($E = mc^2$).
- **General Relativity**: Gravitational lensing, black hole predictions, and spacetime distortion caused by mass.
- **Photoelectric Effect**: Discovery of light quanta (photons) proving the particle nature of electromagnetic radiation.

### 6. 💬 Words to Remember (Quote Block)
- High-contrast charcoal navy backdrop (`#172033`).
- Left: Authentic high-contrast black-and-white portrait of Einstein.
- Right: Large decorative quotation glyph with the famous quote:
  > *“Imagination is more important than knowledge. For knowledge is limited, whereas imagination embraces the entire world.”*
  > <br>— **Albert Einstein**

### 7. 📜 Footer & Educational Attribution
- Citations: Formally credits Wikipedia, Encyclopaedia Britannica, and Nobel Prize resources.
- Media attribution: Formally credits Wikimedia Commons for photographic archives.

## 💻 8. Getting Started Locally

To run this tribute page on your local computer:

1. **Clone the repository:**
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/PriyoGhosh02/OIBSIP.git
   ```

2. **Navigate to the Tribute folder:**
   ```bash
   cd OIBSIP/WebDev-L2-Tribute
   ```

3. **Open in any modern web browser:**
   - Double-click `index.html` to open it in Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox, OR
   - Run a local static server:
     ```bash
     # Using Python 3
     python -m http.server 8000
     ```
   - Visit `http://localhost:8000` in your web browser.
